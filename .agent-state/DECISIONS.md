# DECISIONS LOG

## ADR-023 (2026-08-25): Catalog Artifact Roles and the Identity Invariant

- **Context**: Three catalog files that once agreed on 100 works now hold 100 / 217 / 187 rows, and no document or test stated what their relationship had become. 117 of the 187 public works are absent from the original curated 100, which meant 30 of the launch 100 no longer appear in public — with no recorded explanation. This ADR is a **documentation-only** record established empirically from the artifacts, the projection pipeline and git history; it changes no catalog data.

- **Artifact roles** (verified against `scripts/catalog/project-approved-catalog.ts`, `src/lib/artcovr/catalog-projection.ts`, `catalog-import.ts` and the file readers):
  - `catalog/curated-artworks.json` — **100 rows, frozen historical launch-review candidate set.** Every row carries `rightsApproved: false`. It is *not* an input to publication: no runtime module and no projection step reads it. Its live readers are the approval-workbook tooling (`build-/import-/verify-approval-workbook.mjs`), `curate-launch.ts`, `swap-launch-works.ts`, `validate.ts --scope=review`, `validate-curation-metadata.ts`, and identity/regression tests. It is the candidate-stage baseline that the identity invariant is measured against, nothing more.
  - `catalog/approved-artworks.json` — **217 rows, the owner-approved master audit artifact** and the only publication input. Positions 1–100 are the launch set; positions 101–217 are post-launch expansion.
  - `src/lib/artcovr/curated-public.json` — **187 rows, generated only** by `bun run catalog:project` → `projectApprovedCatalog(approved)`. It is never hand-edited; `catalog:project:check` proves it is in sync.
  - `src/lib/artcovr/curated-review.json` — 169 rows, private-staging snapshot (approved positions 1–169), never a publication source (ADR-006/ADR-011).

- **The invariant** (all verified by set arithmetic over slug / id / sha256, 2026-08-25):
  - `curated (100) ⊂ approved (217)` — identity-preserving: for all 100 slugs the `id`, `sha256` and `position` are byte-identical between the two files, and the launch positions are exactly 1..100.
  - `approved = curated ∪ expansion`, `|expansion| = 117`, every expansion row at `position > 100`.
  - `review (169) ⊂ approved`, occupying approved positions 1..169 contiguously.
  - `public = { r ∈ approved | r.tier ≠ "delete" }` — 217 − 30 = 187, exact set equality both directions.
  - `D = curated \ public` = the **30 `tier: "delete"` rows**, and `D ⊂ curated` — i.e. every delisted work is an original launch work and **no expansion work was ever delisted**. Tier split: launch 100 = 23 featured / 47 archive / 30 delete; expansion 117 = 69 featured / 48 archive / 0 delete; public 187 = 92 featured / 95 archive.
  - `public/assets/artworks` ↔ `public` is a bijection: 187 files, 187 `image` paths, 0 orphans on disk, and 0 of the 30 delete-tier derivatives still present.

- **Fate of the 30**: they were **owner-delisted, not lost or superseded**. Every one is still present, intact and rights-flagged in `approved-artworks.json`; only its display tier is `delete`, which `projectApprovedCatalog` drops from the projection and `prune-deployment-assets.ts` / `verify-export-isolation.ts` then hold out of the export. The tier field and the 23/47/30 launch split were introduced by commit `12bbcd9` ("owner display tiers — featured home, full archive, deletes off", authored by the repo owner, 2026-08-15), whose message states the tiers were "assigned from the owner's recorded verdicts (23/47/30 across the launch set)" under a green/blue/red contact-sheet review, where "red (delete) works leave the site entirely". That commit is **not** in HEAD's ancestry (ADR-019 notes this); the data reached HEAD through PR merges `04184c7` / `e7161f0` / `033d8cc`, and the 23/47/30 split in the current artifact matches its message exactly.
  - **OPEN QUESTION — not invented here**: the per-slug rationale is **not recorded anywhere on disk.** The owner's contact-sheet verdict artifact is not in the repository and not under `E:\ART_COLLECTION\.artcovr-curation\` (`final-100/final-100-id-title-map.json` is only the contact-sheet index; it carries no verdict column). No ADR, `excluded-candidates.json` entry, or swap spec explains *why these particular 30* were red-flagged. The aggregate claim above is the strongest evidence that exists. Do not restore, re-price, or re-publish any of the 30 on the assumption that the deletion was accidental; if the reason matters, ask the owner and record it here. The 30 slugs are pinned in `tests/unit/catalog-coherence.test.ts` via the `curated \ public` identity.

- **Decision**: These roles and relations are the contract. `curated-artworks.json` is frozen history and must not be grown to track the catalog; `approved-artworks.json` is the single publication input; `curated-public.json` is regenerated, never edited. Audit-preserved deletions stay in the approved artifact forever (ADR-022) and are removed from publication by tier alone — never by deleting the row.

- **Impact**: `tests/unit/catalog-coherence.test.ts` (new, 6 tests) pins the subset relations, the identity preservation of the launch 100, the `public = approved \ delete` equality, the "all 30 delisted are launch works" property, and the asset bijection. The four row counts (100 / 217 / 187 / 169) are pinned as *provisional* state markers, commented as such: the catalog is expected to grow, but a change must arrive with a decision record rather than a silent projection re-run.

## ADR-022 (2026-08-21): Audit-Preserved Deletions Are Not Publication Inputs
- **Context**: The owner-approved artifact intentionally retains 30 `tier: "delete"` rows for audit history. Those rows still carry their historical rights/publication booleans, and the Supabase importer previously treated those booleans as sufficient to emit listed database rows. The storefront and storage projection excluded them, so the generated database seed disagreed with the public catalog.
- **Decision**: `buildCatalogImport` excludes delete-tier audit rows before publication validation and SQL/manifest generation. The generated Supabase identity set must exactly equal the non-delete public projection. Publication validation is separate from private owner-review/source validation, and owner-approved batch finalization stages catalog replacements and protected assets as a recoverable local transaction.
- **Impact**: The offline seed now emits exactly 187 publishable identities and zero delete-tier identities while the 217-row owner audit artifact remains intact. This is an offline correction only; no live database, storage, purchase, entitlement, or revocation state was changed.

## ADR-014 (2026-08-14): Native Visual Index — Vector Diversity Sorting, Related Works, Machine Labels
- **Context**: The owner asked for (1) the storefront ordered by image vectors and (2) fastText-style keywords plus all available machine metadata on every one of the 100 works. The owner's existing fastText system (22,133 labelled images, CLIP ViT-B/32 embeddings, 7 task vocabularies in `metadata-atlas/out/meta_ai_schema.json`) has **zero key overlap** with this catalog: it joins on verbatim source `filename` (22,260 unique basenames from the legacy collection), while these 100 works are SHA-locked regenerated/curated originals with ARTCOVR slugs and their own display derivatives. Verified: no ARTCOVR slug, display path, or source SHA appears in `embedding_filenames.json`, `metadata_index.json`, or `fasttext_predictions.js`. Joining is therefore impossible — the equivalents must be computed natively from our own pixels.
- **Decision**: A new, offline-generated, committed artifact pair, generated by `npm run catalog:visual-index` (`scripts/catalog/build-visual-index.ts` → `scripts/catalog/compute-visual-index.py`) and deliberately **not** part of `npm run build`:
  - `src/lib/artcovr/visual-index.json` (128 KB, shipped): per slug — top-6 related slugs with cosine scores, `diversityRank`, and one label per fastText task.
  - `src/lib/artcovr/visual-vectors.json` (690 KB, audit/regeneration only): the 512-d L2-normalized vectors. Site code must never import it (100 × 512 floats in the client bundle for no user-visible gain); `tests/unit/visual-index.test.ts` greps all of `src/**` to enforce that and asserts the shipped file carries no `vector` key.
  - The owner's 7 task vocabularies are vendored verbatim as `scripts/catalog/fasttext-vocabularies.json` (style 6, medium 6, mood 6, category 11, weather 12, colorblend 13, domcolor 9) so regeneration and tests do not depend on a path outside the repo. Every emitted label is filtered against the same governance-banned taxonomy terms as curator keywords (`tests/unit/catalog-curation.test.ts`).
- **Backend deviation (honest naming)**: The intended backend is CLIP ViT-B/32 (`openai/clip-vit-base-patch32` — the same weights the owner's browser gallery used via `Xenova/clip-vit-base-patch32`) with zero-shot text-image scoring. The container that produced the committed artifact could not obtain any model weights: the session egress policy answers 403 to `huggingface.co`, `download.pytorch.org`, `openaipublic.azureedge.net`, S3/GCS, jsDelivr, codeload and `github.com` release downloads; only `pypi.org`, `files.pythonhosted.org`, `registry.npmjs.org` and `raw.githubusercontent.com` are reachable, and no PyPI/npm distribution bundles CLIP weights (all 706 PyPI `*clip*` packages were checked). Rather than label the artifact `clip-*` and lie about its provenance, the committed artifact records what actually produced it:
  - `version: "artcovr-visual-descriptor-v1"` — a deterministic 512-d descriptor computed from the pixels (spatial hue/saturation and luminance histograms, global HSV joint histogram, spatial edge-orientation histograms and edge statistics, multi-scale Laplacian texture energy, CIELAB colour moments, 35 global scalars; blocks individually normalized, whole vector L2-normalized).
  - `labelVersion: "descriptor-rules-v1"` — each label is the argmax of an explicit, auditable scoring rule over measured pixel statistics plus the SHA-locked curator text already in the catalog; confidence is the winner/runner-up margin damped by how much evidence actually matched (mean confidence 0.40–0.56 per task, never a fabricated 0.99).
  - `compute-visual-index.py --backend clip` implements the CLIP path (image tower + zero-shot prompts per task, writing `clip-vitb32-v1` / `clip-zeroshot-v1`) for regeneration on a machine with model access; it is marked unverified because it could not be executed here. Site code, artifact schema, and tests are backend-agnostic: a CLIP regeneration is a drop-in artifact swap.
- **Ordering**: `displayArtworks` now uses the farthest-point traversal (`diversityRank`: start at the vector closest to the centroid, then repeatedly append the work maximizing its minimum cosine distance to everything placed) whenever every displayed work is indexed, and falls back to `balanceDisplayOrder` otherwise. Measured against the category round-robin over the same vectors: worst adjacent similarity 0.873 (was 0.875), mean max-similarity within a ±3 grid window 0.739 (was 0.760); the bowl cluster that used to sit side by side separates from 1 to 85 positions (`city-in-the-broth`/`staircase-soup`) and 11 to 74 (`staircase-soup`/`storm-in-a-fishbowl`). FPT optimizes distance to all already-placed works, so it improves worst-case and local-window adjacency rather than guaranteeing every pair separates.
- **Integration**: machine label strings join `buildArtworkSearchText` (archive queries like "minimalism", "film noir shadowy", "foggy" now hit); product pages render a static server-rendered "Related works" section (up to 4 neighbours, restricted to works present in `displayArtworks`, no client JS); archive cards show the style label subtly. Ordering-pinned tests (`displayArtworks.slice(13)`, `% displayArtworks.length`) are source-text contracts and stay green unchanged.
- **Impact**: 137 unit tests green (13 new), typecheck, lint, production build with prune + export-isolation gates green (`{"publishedAssets":100,"removedUnpublishedAssets":0}`, `{"scannedFiles":1953,"forbiddenSlugs":0,"violations":0}`). Artifact regeneration is byte-deterministic (verified by re-running the builder). Owner follow-up: re-run `npm run catalog:visual-index -- --backend clip` on a machine with model access to upgrade the vectors and labels to true CLIP zero-shot output.

## ADR-016 (2026-08-15): Launch-Selection SHA Identity Invariant — Empirically Valid, Regression Test Added

- **Context**: ULTRAPAN task 3 reported `"19 of 100 rows carry a sourceSha256 that matches no sha256 in catalog/curated-artworks.json. All 19 are generated_images. The other 81 match, including all 8 regenerated_originals (which were the ones expected to differ)`. Investigation revealed the observation was a misread of the reverted rescore-proposal variant's emitted launch-selection (which DID emit `sourceSha256` for `generated_images`). In the canonical launch-selection (`src/lib/artcovr/launch-selection.ts`), the 19 `generated_images` entries use `sourceOrdinal` (1-123), NOT `sourceSha256` — the XOR invariant asserted by `tests/unit/catalog-curation.test.ts:31-32` (`Boolean(sourceOrdinal) !== Boolean(sourceSha256)`). The four `sourceSha256`-bearing pools are `new_meta_images` (12), `meta_updated_images` (1), `concept_reference_art` (60), and `regenerated_originals` (8) — totalling 81 entries, exactly the count ULTRAPAN's check found.
- **Empirical result**: every `sourceSha256` value in the canonical `launchSelection` is present as a `sha256` value in `catalog/curated-artworks.json` (81/81 match, 0 unmatched). `curated-artworks.json.sha256` is the source file hash per ADR-010 ("Catalog sha256 fields track source files, not display derivatives, so re-encoding is identity-safe"), so the invariant holds for the canonical selection: every launch-selection row that identifies its source by hash resolves to a row in the curated catalog with that same hash.
- **Decision**: The `generated_images` pool uses `sourceOrdinal` (file index into `generated_images/style_library_manifest.json`) rather than `sourceSha256` because the ordinal-level identity is the source-of-truth for that pool (its `curate-launch.ts:344-346` lookup is `normalizeGenerated(selection.sourceOrdinal)`, not `metaByHash.get(...)`). The pool-by-hash lookup is reserved for pools whose members are not stable by ordinal (e.g., where renaming/reordering is acceptable). A new regression test (`tests/unit/catalog-launch-identity.test.ts`) asserts the canonical invariant: every `launchSelection` entry that has `sourceSha256` MUST appear as a `sha256` value in `catalog/curated-artworks.json`, AND every entry has exactly one of `sourceOrdinal`/`sourceSha256`. The test fails loudly if either invariant breaks (e.g., a future proposal tries to re-introduce `sourceSha256` on `generated_images` without updating `curated-artworks.json`).
- **Impact**: Task 3 of the ULTRAPAN is CLOSED with a regression test. No real identity break exists in the rights-gated catalog; the published manifest (`curated-public.json`) carries no `sha256`/`sourceSha256` field at all so it cannot drift.

## ADR-017 (2026-08-15): Pricing Deferred — Fabricated Placeholder Prices, No Owner Price List Yet

## ADR-018 (2026-08-15): Rights/Approval Reconciliation — Stale Candidate Flags Stripped From the Approved Artifact

- **Context**: 60 of 100 rows in `catalog/approved-artworks.json` carried `reviewFlags: ["commercial_rights_unconfirmed","owner_approval_required"]` simultaneously with `rightsApproved === true` — a direct contradiction (the flags assert rights unconfirmed; the field asserts approved). The flags were copied verbatim from `catalog/curated-artworks.json` (the pre-approval CANDIDATE set, where every row is `rightsApproved === false` and those same flags are the LEGITIMATE candidate-stage notes) by the 2026-08-14 owner-directed swap (`catalog/swaps/2026-08-14-style-diversity-swap.json`), which set `rightsApproved === true` but never cleared the inherited candidate-stage flags. The `approval-import-report.json` recorded `approved: 0` / `rejectedOrPending: 100` / `launchCountValid: false` / `committed: false` / `blockers: ["EMPTY_APPROVAL_SET"]`.
