## 1. The decisive product idea

Do not build another template shop with an AI generator attached.

Build an **artist-led, rights-reviewed cover-art platform**:

> Artist-led artwork. AI-personalized for your release. Rights you can actually understand.

The public experience should feel like a contemporary gallery, a premium record shop, and a cultural music platform. Behind it sits a serious creative studio where a customer can license one of Sergey's original artworks, audition it against their release, create controlled descendants, typeset it professionally, compare versions, collaborate with their team, and export a release-ready package with a clear license and provenance record.

The moat is not the image model. Models will keep changing. The moat is:

> **curation + verified source rights + protected artistic DNA + controlled transformation + truthful scarcity + exact licensing + release-ready delivery**

### The positioning gap

Today's market is split:

- Premade stores sell speed and scarcity, but offer limited creative control after purchase. Cover Art Market emphasizes one-time exclusive covers and 24-hour delivery; PremadePixels currently anchors much of the low market around roughly $15–$80. [Cover Art Market](https://www.coverartmarket.com/), [PremadePixels](https://www.premadepixels.com/)
- AI cover generators sell cheap flexibility, but generally cannot provide strong provenance, meaningful exclusivity, or reliable professional typography.

The opportunity is between them: **a real artwork foundation, personalized through a rights-aware AI studio, with the exact final export registered to one release.**

## 2. What the high-end references actually teach us

### Okay Dev

[Okay Dev](https://okaydev.co/) is not one designer; it is a creator-first portfolio, gallery, directory, and social platform. Its useful lessons are structural:

- The work comes before the platform chrome.
- Makers, collaborators, tools, tags, process, and context remain attached to every project.
- Expressive filters make discovery feel cultural rather than transactional.
- Motion demonstrates capability instead of decorating a claim.

Apply that here by making every cover inseparable from its source artwork, creator story, visual DNA, editable boundaries, license state, and derivative lineage.

### Brunello Cucinelli's 2026 AI commerce experience

Makemepulse describes Brunello Cucinelli's new commerce experience as intent-led and dynamically assembled, with AI operating quietly through a contextual prompt rather than presenting a generic chatbot. [Makemepulse case study](https://www.makemepulse.com/case-study/brunello-cucinelli-ai-shopping-experience)

Apply that here with a persistent intent bar:

> “Show me cold nocturnal covers with no faces, low visual density, room for a long title, under $175.”

The interface should reorganize normal, inspectable catalog results. Conventional browse, back behavior, stable URLs, and filters must remain available.

### No Art, Obys, and music-culture experiences

[No Art](https://www.noartmusic.com/) combines releases, events, gallery, label, map, story, and commerce inside one music-and-art universe. [Obys Experiment Space](https://experiment.obys.agency/) treats unfinished concepts and experiments as a living archive.

Apply that here through editorial drops, process fragments, artist notes, curated visual worlds, and release stories. The store should feel inside music culture—not like a detached SaaS catalog.

### Pangram Pangram, Krea, and FLORA

Typeface commerce provides a useful behavioral analogy: a buyer wants to try the material before licensing it. Pangram Pangram lets visitors manipulate type before choosing a license. [Pangram Pangram](https://pangrampangram.com/)

Modern creative tools such as [Krea](https://www.krea.ai/blog) and [FLORA](https://flora.ai/) demonstrate the importance of direct manipulation, branches, comparison, reusable workflows, and multi-model flexibility. The lesson is not to copy their complexity. It is to avoid a chat-only editor that hides state and makes versions difficult to compare.

### Bonhomme and Avant Arte

A 2026 Bonhomme profile argues that interaction should translate what the brand actually is, with design and development evolving as one discipline rather than adding effects after the interface is complete. [Codrops: The Story Is in the Interaction](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/08/03/the-story-is-in-the-interaction-bonhommes-digital-experiences-for-luxury-brands/)

Apply that here by making the persistent artwork square, the Artist Boundary, the Take lineage, and the Edition Seal express the actual business model. They are not decorations: they show source authorship, permitted transformation, version history, and contractual registration.

[Avant Arte](https://avantarte.com/about) demonstrates that premium art commerce is strengthened by artist storytelling, process visibility, controlled editions, and confidence in the production object—not by hiding the mechanics of collecting. Its guide also treats a certificate of authenticity as a provenance document accompanying an editioned artwork. [Avant Arte edition guide](https://avantarte.com/insights/guides/what-is-an-edition)

Apply that here through curated Drops, process/source records, truthful edition counts, a readable rights receipt, and a permanent license/provenance Vault.

### One-sentence experience north star

> A cover-art store that feels like discovering a future classic in a record shop, lets you audition it like a professional art director, and then opens into a provenance-safe AI studio where every variation remains connected to the original artist and license.

## 3. Product framework

| Layer | Product promise | Customer result |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery | Search by feeling, sound, image, and visual language | “I found art that actually fits my record.” |
| Curation | Every foundation is artist-led and rights-reviewed; only documented Green works receive a verified human-authored badge | “This does not feel like generic AI slop.” |
| Licensing | Plain-English, project-bound rights | “I know exactly what I can do with it.” |
| Creation | AI can change only what the artist permits | “The result is mine without erasing the source artist.” |
| Typography | Real editable type, not baked-in generative lettering | “My title is correct, legible, and professional.” |
| Trust | Immutable masters, version lineage, hashes, and certificates | “My exact final is documented and will not be intentionally reissued.” |
| Delivery | Current DSP checks and complete campaign packages | “I can release this immediately.” |

### Core objects

1. **Master Artwork** — Sergey's immutable, highest-quality source file.
2. **Rights Record** — ownership evidence, contributors, releases, third-party assets, previous uses, and restrictions.
3. **Visual DNA Profile** — mood, palette, density, contrast, era, texture, composition, realism, genre affinity, and typography space.
4. **Edit Permission Map** — protected elements, editable regions, allowed transformations, prohibited uses, and maximum deviation.
5. **Project** — one customer's named music release.
6. **Version Node** — an immutable child in the project's visual version graph.
7. **Exact Export** — the approved final image registered to that release and checksum.
8. **License Grant** — the accepted transaction-specific rights and restrictions.
9. **Credit Ledger** — an immutable record of holds, charges, releases, refunds, and source wallet.
10. **Export Pack** — final static, motion, metadata, license, and provenance files.

Never overwrite the master or a prior version. Every edit creates a child node.

## 4. The signature customer journey

### 1. Begin with intent, not a category menu

The visitor can:

- describe a visual feeling;
- browse a curated drop;
- upload an image reference;
- in a later phase, optionally upload a private 15–30 second song excerpt;
- use conventional mood, color, genre, composition, and price filters.

Omit audio analysis from Phase 1. For Phase 2, choose a commercially licensed browser analyzer or explicitly accept and satisfy its open-source obligations. In particular, do not ship Essentia.js in a proprietary SaaS until counsel approves AGPL-3.0 compliance or a commercial license is obtained. [Essentia.js licensing](https://essentia.upf.edu/licensing_information.html), [Essentia.js repository](https://github.com/MTG/essentia.js)

Do not download or alter Spotify content. Spotify's current audio-analysis endpoints have faced access and deprecation changes, and its developer policies prohibit downloading its content. A pasted Spotify link can be used only for permitted metadata or a licensed analysis provider—not stream ripping. [Spotify developer policy context](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api)

### 2. Convert sound and language into Visual DNA

Translate the brief into understandable axes:

- warm ↔ cold;
- intimate ↔ monumental;
- still ↔ kinetic;
- sparse ↔ dense;
- clean ↔ distressed;
- literal ↔ abstract;
- soft ↔ confrontational;
- contemporary ↔ archival;
- portrait ↔ environment ↔ object ↔ typographic;
- low ↔ high title-space availability.

Show why each result appears: “Nocturnal palette, restrained analog texture, low-density composition, and strong upper-title space.” Do not expose an unexplained or falsely precise black-box score.

### 3. Enter the Cover Audition Room

Every artwork detail page should provide:

- untouched, large artwork;
- source story and creator/process context;
- current license state and remaining edition count;
- previous license count without exposing buyers' private data;
- live artist-name and release-title typesetting;
- Spotify/Apple thumbnail, vinyl sleeve, cassette, phone, poster, and story previews; add Canvas/motion in Phase 2;
- 48 px, 96 px, grayscale, contrast, and safe-crop tests;
- no audio in Phase 1; optional user-controlled private playback can arrive later and must never autoplay;
- plain-English license comparison with the full price visible before checkout;
- `Audition with your release`, `License as shown`, `Request an artist edit`, and `Save` actions; after personalization the primary CTA becomes `Secure this edition`. Add `Share approval room` in Phase 2.

Anonymous Phase 1 audition includes watermarked typography, crop/position, scale proofing, context previews, and the Artist Boundary overlay. Do not expose unrestricted AI generation or a commercially usable derivative before licensing. Show two or three curator-made examples of permitted transformation directions.

### 4. License, then direct in Cover Studio

After the license is confirmed, the source artwork remains a visible locked root. The customer can:

- make a natural-language semantic edit;
- brush/lasso a region for inpainting;
- preserve subject, face, composition, palette, or texture;
- reframe/outpaint for other formats in a later phase;
- provide role-labeled references: subject, face, style, structure, palette, texture;
- create a palette or lighting variation where permitted;
- compare two separate versions at the same crop and thumbnail size; add four-up interface comparison later if it remains useful;
- branch, fork, undo, and return to the licensed root;
- add real editable typography as a separate vector/canvas layer;
- see the exact credit quote before generation.

One generation result must be one standalone image file. Do not generate collages, grids, contact sheets, split screens, mood boards, or multiple scenes inside a single output. The interface may display several separate result files side by side for comparison.

### 5. Approve and export

Run automatic checks for:

- correct dimensions and 1:1 ratio;
- sRGB/RGB conversion;
- title and artist metadata agreement;
- OCR accuracy and missing/extra text;
- unintended logos, watermarks, or artifacts;
- protected-region preservation;
- edit locality;
- small-thumbnail legibility;
- safe crop;
- flashing/motion safety when relevant;
- provider provenance signal detection.

The buyer then locks an exact export and receives the release package, license certificate, provenance manifest, and permanent project vault.

## 5. Licensing architecture

Do not lead with **lease**. It sounds temporary and commodity-like. Customer-facing actions should say **License this artwork**, **Secure this edition**, and **Direct your version**. A FAQ can explain that some music marketplaces informally call project-bound licensing “leasing,” but the product, contract, and checkout should consistently say **license**.

### A. Limited Release License — main customer offer

Internal/legal record: **Numbered Foundation License**.

- One named single, EP, album, mixtape, or podcast release.
- Worldwide and perpetual for that named release.
- Unlimited DSP streams and digital release promotion.
- Authorized platform-created descendants permitted.
- Exact approved final export registered to the buyer and never intentionally reissued.
- The underlying foundation remains available only for a disclosed limited edition, ideally three or five licenses.
- Show `Edition 2 of 5`, prior count, and remaining availability.
- No raw-master redistribution, standalone artwork resale, model training, trademark registration, or use on another release.
- Necessary use by the customer's label, distributor, printer, and campaign partners is allowed for the named release.

Do not cap music streams. Stream caps are hard to monitor and punish success.

### B. Campaign Extension

Internal/legal record: **Campaign License**.

Everything in Foundation, plus:

- connected merchandise allowance;
- higher physical-unit allowance;
- social campaign formats;
- Spotify Canvas and Apple motion options;
- more Studio credits;
- team/label approval room;
- expanded paid-campaign rights.

### C. Exclusive Music License

Internal/legal record: **Music-Field Exclusive License**.

Available only while a master has never been licensed.

- Exclusive, worldwide, perpetual rights within the music-release field.
- Atomically reserve it during the order flow and retire it after confirmed payment and signature.
- Include authorized descendants, campaign, merchandise, and transferable source components where permitted.
- Sergey's retained physical-original, exhibition, archival, and portfolio-display rights must be explicit.
- Do not issue this tier through ordinary click-to-buy checkout. Payment creates a pending order; the license becomes effective only after Sergey signs a counsel-approved electronic exclusive-license instrument identifying the work and exclusive rights granted.
- Do not use “acquisition” or “ownership” unless copyright is actually assigned.
- Full copyright assignment is a separate counsel-drafted transaction, not default checkout boilerplate.

Under U.S. law, an exclusive license is a transfer of copyright ownership and generally requires a written instrument signed by the rights owner. [U.S. Copyright Office Circular 12](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ12.pdf)

Once the first numbered license sells, disable the one-of-one option. Do not later call the work unique while earlier licenses remain alive.

### D. Bespoke Art Direction / Full Assignment

For labels and premium clients:

- custom creative direction;
- full visual campaign world;
- negotiated source delivery;
- physical original priced separately;
- separate electronic signature and transaction-specific agreement.

Owning a physical artwork or local file does not itself transfer copyright. [U.S. Copyright Act, Chapter 2](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap2.html)

### Truthful AI-rights language

Do not promise “100% unique,” “guaranteed copyrighted,” or “full ownership with no legal concerns.” The U.S. Copyright Office states that copyright protects sufficient human-authored expression but not purely AI-generated material or material without enough human control. [U.S. Copyright Office AI copyrightability report](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf)

Use wording like:

> You receive the commercial license described for the selected artwork and its authorized exports, to the extent those rights exist. We will not intentionally reissue the exact licensed export. Generative outputs may contain noncopyrightable elements and may resemble outputs created for others.

Exclusivity can attach to Sergey's human-authored foundation, the approved exact composition, and the platform's contractual no-reissue commitment. It cannot honestly attach to a broad style, idea, mood, genre, or prompt.

### Suggested pricing tests

These are positioning tests—not validated prices:

| Offer | Starting test range |
|---|---:|
| Archive Limited Release, 1 of 5 | $149–$195 |
| Signature Limited Release, 1 of 3 | $225–$325 |
| Campaign License total | $350–$650 |
| Never-before-licensed Exclusive Music License | From $1,500; artwork and scope dependent |
| Artist's Eye Review | Limited-capacity paid add-on |
| Bespoke art direction / campaign world | $2,500–$7,500+ |
| Full assignment / physical original | Custom, materially above exclusive license |

Do not turn Sergey's artwork into a cheap subscription library. Membership should reduce compute cost and add team workflow; it should not erase the value of the artwork license.

## 6. Credit system and unit economics

Keep the artwork license and AI compute wallet separate. A license must remain valid and downloadable even after a subscription ends or credits run out.

Maintain two ledgers:

1. Customer-facing Studio Credits.
2. Actual provider cost in USD microcents.

Suggested launch meter:

| Action | Studio Credits |
|---|---:|
| Crop, resize, typography, layout, undo, compliance preview | 0 |
| Quick 1K draft | 1 |
| Standard 1K generation or edit | 3 |
| Studio 2K generation/edit | 5 |
| Master 4K generation/edit | 8 |
| Reference-heavy premium final | 10 |
| Conservative 2× upscale | 2 |
| Conservative 4× upscale | 4 |

For the Phase 1 experience, include 12 Studio Credits with the Limited Release License—approximately four standard 3-credit masked edits. Activate the free deterministic tools and the standard edit first; keep quick, 2K/4K, reference-heavy, and upscale tiers as measured experiments until quality and COGS benchmarks are reliable.

Rules:

- Quote the exact amount before the user submits.
- Reserve credits atomically before the provider job.
- Settle once after a valid result passes required checks.
- Automatically release the hold for moderation blocks, provider failures, timeouts, malformed outputs, or failed mandatory QA.
- Never double-charge webhook retries.
- Keep purchased and promotional/subscription wallets separate; spend expiring promotional credits first.
- No automatic top-up by default. If later enabled, require separate opt-in, a spending cap, and warnings.
- Charge for generated outputs, not conversational clarification.
- Exporting already licensed standard files should not become a surprise toll.
- Show an outcome estimate: “approximately 12 standard edits,” not only a large arbitrary credit number.

OpenAI's current image guide illustrates why the app needs its own stable meter: `gpt-image-2` square output is documented around $0.006 low, $0.053 medium, and $0.211 high before reference-input costs, while provider prices and model routes can change. [OpenAI image generation and pricing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation)

Use Stripe Checkout Sessions for one-time licenses and credit packs. If recurring Studio plans are added, use Stripe Billing plus Checkout and the Customer Portal. Do not build manual renewal loops. [Stripe Checkout](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/checkout), [Stripe Billing integration](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/designing-integration), [Stripe Customer Portal](https://docs.stripe.com/customer-management/integrate-customer-portal)

## 7. AI and editing architecture

### Launch principle

Use one primary provider through a clean adapter in the private Phase 1 vertical slice. Before a public paid launch, add one benchmarked fallback or a clearly defined human recovery path so a provider outage does not strand licensed customers. Do not expose a confusing model marketplace to the buyer.

### Recommended provider roles

| Need | Recommended path |
|---|---|
| Conversational and masked source-preserving edits | OpenAI `gpt-image-2` through Image/Responses APIs |
| Reference-heavy composition and 1K–4K workflows | Gemini 3.1 Flash Image / Gemini 3 Pro Image |
| Low-cost drafts, palette, text experiments, pinned versions | FLUX.2 |
| Explicit structure/style strength, fill/expand, custom models, conservative upscale | Adobe Firefly Services |
| Focused erase, background, recolor, structure, sketch utilities | Stability Image Services |

OpenAI's Responses API supports multi-turn image editing, and its Image API supports existing-image edits, reference images, and masks. OpenAI also warns that exact text placement and structured composition can still fail. [OpenAI image guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation)

Google currently documents multi-turn image workflows, 1K–4K output, improved text, and up to fourteen reference images across its Gemini 3 image family. [Gemini image generation](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/image-generation)

FLUX.2 provides multi-reference editing and output up to 4 MP. [FLUX.2 image editing](https://docs.bfl.ai/flux_2/flux2_image_editing)

Adobe exposes independent structure and style references with strength controls, plus fill, expand, upscale, and custom models. [Adobe structure reference](https://developer.adobe.com/firefly-services/docs/firefly-api/guides/concepts/structure-image-reference/), [Adobe style reference](https://developer.adobe.com/firefly-services/docs/firefly-api/guides/concepts/style-image-reference/)

### The non-negotiable typography decision

Artist name, release title, advisory marks, legal copy, and layout must remain separate editable SVG/Canvas layers using properly licensed fonts. AI may suggest typographic directions, create texture masks, or produce lettering references, but the final exact title should not depend on generative raster text.

### Normalized generation request

```json
{
  "operation": "generate|semantic_edit|masked_edit|outpaint|upscale",
  "project_id": "...",
  "parent_version_id": "...",
  "base_asset_id": "...",
  "user_prompt": "...",
  "compiled_prompt": "...",
  "locked_elements": ["face", "composition", "signature texture"],
  "mask_asset_id": "...",
  "references": [
    {"asset_id": "...", "role": "style", "strength": 65}
  ],
  "palette": ["#0B0B10", "#D14B3F"],
  "output": {"aspect": "1:1", "resolution": "1K", "quality": "standard"},
  "quote_version": "2026-08-01"
}
```

Every output must retain:

- parent and source hashes;
- user instruction and compiled prompt;
- provider, exact model/snapshot, endpoint, and parameters;
- references, roles, masks, and locked regions;
- dimensions, format, profile, output checksum;
- provider request ID, latency, COGS, and charged credits;
- moderation result and stable failure code;
- OCR, preservation, locality, palette, and approval scores;
- provenance verification result;
- separate typography/layout JSON.

### Do not promise

- perfect preservation outside a mask unless unchanged original pixels are deterministically composited back;
- exact fonts or kerning from raster generation;
- recreation from a seed after a provider silently updates a model;
- real-time premium 4K editing—use a progressive draft and asynchronous final render;
- automatic originality/copyright clearance;
- meaningful custom style training from one source image;
- infinite provider-hosted retention—immediately copy accepted outputs into owned storage.

### Provenance

Preserve provider provenance signals, record application lineage, and verify after final compositing. The C2PA standard provides interoperable, cryptographically verifiable content history. [C2PA specification](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.4/specs/C2PA_Specification.html)

Google applies SynthID to generated imagery; Adobe applies Content Credentials to Firefly content. Where commercially and technically available, sign final exports with an application-issued C2PA manifest referencing parent hashes. A `not detected` result must never be presented as proof that an image is human-made.

## 8. Experience design system

### The governing idea: “The Living Sleeve”

This should not feel like a marketing site that hands the customer off to a dashboard. It should feel like entering a digital record atelier and carrying one piece of art through a complete ritual:

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    A["Archive<br/>Find the world"] --> B["Sleeve Room<br/>See your release"]
    B --> C["Studio<br/>Direct your version"]
    C --> D["Vault<br/>Register and release"]
```

The emotional product is not “an AI image.” It is the moment a musician sees their real artist name and release title on an artwork that already feels culturally convincing—and then discovers that they can direct it without destroying the hand behind it.

The same square artwork is the continuous object across the whole product. It begins as an image in the Archive, enlarges into the Sleeve Room, passes behind the rights receipt at checkout, becomes the Studio canvas, and resolves into the registered release package in the Vault. The interface changes around the square; the square does not appear to teleport between unrelated templates.

This shared-object continuity is the signature experience and the first design principle to protect during implementation.

Use a consistent creative-production vocabulary, paired with plain labels until customers learn it:

`SOURCE MASTER → TAKE → FINAL CUT → EDITION`

- **Archive** — Sergey's rights-reviewed source artwork.
- **Drop** — a tightly curated body of work.
- **Source Master** — the immutable original.
- **Take** — one standalone generated/edited image file.
- **Final Cut** — the approved visual and typography state.
- **Edition** — the licensed slot for a named release.
- **Vault** — the permanent license, lineage, project, and export record.

### Experience laws

1. **The artwork owns attention.** Public UI chrome is visually quieter than the art.
2. **The artwork may lend color; it may not break the interface.** Use one manually approved, contrast-checked accent recorded during ingestion while navigation, body text, pricing, errors, and purchase actions retain stable semantic colors.
3. **One canvas, one take.** Every generated result is one standalone image/file and one continuous composition—never a collage, contact sheet, grid, mood board, split screen, or several concepts baked into one image.
4. **AI is an art-direction instrument, not a chatbot mascot.** No floating bot bubble, glowing orb, generic prompt page, or model-first language.
5. **The source is sacred.** The untouched master is an immutable, named root and always recoverable.
6. **Every AI action is scoped, quoted, reversible, and attributable.** The user sees what may change, what must remain, the credit cost, and the parent version before running it.
7. **Motion explains continuity or consequence.** It shows where the artwork moved, what changed, what became selected, or what was registered. Decorative motion does not compete with the art.
8. **Evocative language never replaces transactional clarity.** “Sleeve Room” can coexist with “License this cover — $175.”
9. **Trust lives beside the artwork.** Edition truth, edit boundaries, source/process disclosure, full price, and rights summary are never hidden on a corporate trust page.
10. **Details are summoned.** Provenance, model logs, hashes, full agreements, and technical controls remain available without dominating the default journey.
11. **Mobile is recomposed, not miniaturized.** The mobile editor is a canvas plus sequential sheets, not three desktop columns squeezed onto a phone.
12. **No manufactured magic.** No fake scarcity, fake progress, fake match percentages, fake collectors, fake reviews, fake payment success, or fake uniqueness.

### The experiential arc

| Stage | Desired feeling | Dominant object | Primary action | What stays quiet |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Arrival | Intrigue | One commanding cover | Enter the drop / describe the record | Features, plans, technical AI copy |
| Archive | Guided serendipity | A finite curated body of work | Inspect a work | Badges, price clutter, endless feed mechanics |
| Sleeve Room | Recognition | Their name on the cover | Audition with your release | Generative controls |
| Rights receipt | Confidence | Artwork plus exact edition/license | Secure this edition | Legal jargon and upsell pressure |
| Studio | Creative authority | Licensed root and current take | Direct the next change | Provider/model complexity |
| Preflight | Finality | Selected cover at release scale | Lock registered cover | New creative suggestions |
| Vault | Ownership and continuity | Delivered cover, certificate, lineage | Download / start another release | Dashboard statistics |

The experience should become progressively calmer. Discovery can be atmospheric; the transaction becomes plain and exact; the Studio becomes focused; delivery becomes almost ceremonial.

### Two material states

Use one coherent system with two purposeful states rather than one theme per page:

**Gallery state — desire and discovery**

- near-black field;
- artwork illuminated at meaningful scale;
- sparse ivory type;
- limited metadata;
- one art-derived accent;
- motion limited to shared-object movement and subtle focus changes.

**Worktable state — direction and trust**

- warm uncoated-paper field;
- near-black ink;
- restrained aluminum/gray utility surfaces;
- visible guides, hairlines, edition records, and exact controls;
- minimal shadow; hierarchy comes from scale, line, and overlap.

Recommended starting tokens, to be tested against Sergey's actual artwork:

| Token | Value | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `--ink` | `#0B0B0A` | Primary dark shell and text |
| `--paper` | `#F2EFE7` | Worktable/editorial surface |
| `--paper-bright` | `#FAF8F2` | Focused reading and agreements |
| `--graphite` | `#191917` | Studio secondary surface |
| `--metal` | `#A6A69F` | Quiet metadata and inactive controls |
| `--line-dark` | `rgba(242,239,231,.18)` | Dark-state dividers |
| `--line-light` | `rgba(11,11,10,.16)` | Light-state dividers |
| `--danger` | `#C74836` | Stable error/destructive meaning |
| `--success` | `#3F6A50` | Stable completion meaning |
| `--art-accent` | Per artwork | Selection, focus, and small atmospheric details only |

Do not use purple/blue AI gradients, neon light, default glassmorphism, gold luxury clichés, or multiple competing artwork-derived colors. If an approved accent fails contrast in a particular context, use it decoratively and fall back to stable ink/paper for controls.

Store three manually approved artwork display tokens during ingestion: `accent_ink`, `mat_tone`, and `light_accent`. Automatic palette extraction may suggest them, but Sergey or the curator approves the final values. Never tint or filter the source artwork to make it match the interface.

### Typography

Use three licensed roles, not a large font collection:

- **Editorial display:** a high-contrast, expressive face for large cultural statements and drop titles. Recommended direction: PP Editorial New or an equivalently authored display serif.
- **Interface grotesk:** a precise, neutral face with excellent small-size behavior. Recommended direction: Neue Montreal or ABC Diatype.
- **Record mono:** a disciplined mono for edition numbers, rights records, job status, checksums, and technical metadata. Recommended direction: Berkeley Mono or an equivalent.

Obtain the correct web/app licenses before launch. Open-source fallbacks can be Instrument Serif, Geist, and IBM Plex Mono during noncommercial concepting, but the final font decision must be made from screenshots using Sergey's real art.

Recommended type behavior:

- display: fluid `clamp(3.5rem, 8vw, 8rem)`, tight leading, no decorative pretitle;
- section headline: `clamp(2rem, 4vw, 4.5rem)`;
- reading copy: 16–18px, 1.45–1.6 line height, 55–68 character measure;
- interface copy: 13–15px with deliberately set weight/leading;
- metadata: 11–13px mono, never the sole place essential rights are explained;
- buttons: short sentence case or restrained uppercase only when it is part of the brand voice.

The customer's artist and release title must always be rendered with real editable fonts in SVG/Canvas layers. Generated raster lettering may be inspirational texture but is never the authoritative release title.

### Grid and spatial behavior

- Desktop: 12 columns; 32–56px outer gutters; 12–24px inter-column gap; maximum designed width around 1600px.
- Tablet: 6 columns; 24–32px gutters.
- Mobile: 4 columns; 16–20px gutters.
- The square artwork is the controlling module. Major alignments should resolve to its edges, centerline, or title-safe zones.
- Public pages use open fields, rails, and editorial bands instead of nested cards.
- Corners remain predominantly square or 2–4px; larger radii require a functional reason.
- Shadows are rare. Use overlap, surface color, hairlines, and scale to create depth.
- Price, edition, and badges never permanently cover the art.
- A 64px “truth thumbnail” remains available in the Sleeve Room and Studio so a beautiful large composition is constantly tested at streaming scale.

### The first viewport: three doors, not a hero deck

Artwork should occupy roughly 70–80% of the visual emphasis. The first viewport is already a working discovery surface.

Required elements:

- one dominant real Sergey artwork;
- `[BRAND_NAME]` wordmark;
- minimal navigation: `Archive`, `Drops`, `Licensing`, `Studio`;
- one plain-language statement: **START WITH ART. DIRECT WHAT COMES NEXT.**
- supporting copy: **License an original work from Sergey's archive, shape an approved descendant for your release, and lock one documented final.**
- an intent field: **What should this record feel like?**
- three paths: **Enter the latest drop**, **Describe the record**, **Browse the archive**;
- quiet artwork index/availability;
- a glimpse of the next visual state below the fold.

Do not place pricing plans, product-feature cards, fake metrics, testimonials, a dashboard mockup, or a “meet your AI” explanation in the first viewport. The art must create desire before the system explains itself.

### Signature interactions

#### 1. Shared Sleeve

When a work is opened, animate the same DOM/media object into its Sleeve Room position using a shared-element/View Transition when supported. Use a restrained crossfade and geometric reposition fallback. The transition should take approximately 280–420ms and remain interruptible.

#### 2. Name-on-Art moment

Before checkout, the visitor can type their real artist name and release title directly onto a watermarked audition. This is the central conversion moment. Track `NAME_ON_ART_COMPLETED`; it is more meaningful than a generic product-view event.

#### 3. Proofing Rail and Truth Thumbnail

A calibrated rail moves the same cover through `3000px → 300px → 96px → 48px → vinyl sleeve → phone → poster → vertical story`. A persistent 64px square lets the visitor judge immediate streaming readability. The artwork and title treatment remain continuous while only the viewing condition changes. Every stop has a conventional button and keyboard/touch path; this is not a drag-only carousel.

#### 4. Edit Boundary Reveal

A hold/press or labeled control reveals the permitted edit map: protected areas remain crisp; editable areas receive one quiet hatch or tint. Never communicate permission through color alone.

#### 5. Take Lineage

The backend uses an immutable graph, but the default UI is a simple filmstrip: `Original`, `Take 1`, `Selected`, `Delivered`. “Explore from here” reveals branching only when the user needs it.

#### 6. Rights Receipt

Before Stripe, place a readable paper-like rights receipt beside—not over—the artwork. After a brief ordinary panel reveal, the checkout/rights surface remains visually still so comprehension is never competing with atmosphere. It states the named release, license, edition number, total price, included credits/files, exact allowed uses, key restrictions, refund/failure behavior, and exact-export promise.

#### 7. Edition Seal / registration moment

Only after verified payment, assign the edition number, license version, final version code, and export checksum. Show a sub-one-second mechanical deboss/stamp transition outside the artwork: **Edition 2 of 5 registered to [RELEASE].** The square moves from the archival sleeve into the Studio. The seal is never baked into the cover. This is not a loader and has a reduced-motion static version.

#### 8. Artist's Eye Review

Offer a limited-capacity human review in which Sergey personally evaluates the selected direction before final lock. This is genuine human scarcity and a far stronger premium differentiator than more AI generations.

### Discovery: finite, explainable, and cultural

Discovery has three entry systems:

1. **Drops** — editorially sequenced visual worlds with source stories and a clear beginning/end.
2. **Archive Wall** — efficient visual scanning by composition, palette, density, title space, medium, mood, and real availability.
3. **Creative brief** — natural-language intent translated into a finite curated sequence, not an opaque feed.

Provide two archive modes:

- **Wall:** fast visual scan, keyboard navigation, compact metadata on focus, exact URL filters, restored scroll position.
- **Focus:** one work occupies most of the viewport; arrow, wheel, or swipe advances while the user's brief remains persistent.

Do not show an infinite feed. A result should feel selected, not produced. Replace fake precision such as “97% match” with a short explanation:

> Strong dark-to-light separation, restrained texture, open upper title field, and clear 64px silhouette.

Every artwork retains a canonical URL. Quick views can exist, but the entire product must not become a chain of modals.

### Sleeve Room: the real product page

The artwork detail page is not a card enlarged into a Shopify template. It is a fitting room for the release.

Default composition:

- 65–75% visual stage for the untouched artwork;
- 25–35% quiet inspector containing source story, process, edition truth, price, license summary, edit permission, and one CTA;
- persistent truth thumbnail;
- deterministic artist/title controls;
- context rail for streaming library, phone player, vinyl sleeve, poster, square social, and vertical story;
- original/textless toggle;
- clearly labeled edit-boundary reveal.

CTA progression:

1. **Audition with your release**
2. After name/title entry: **Secure this edition**

Anonymous audition may provide typography, crop, positioning, and watermarked context previews. Do not provide unrestricted generative editing before licensing in Phase 1. Show two or three curator-created examples of permitted transformation directions instead.

### Commerce as a rights ritual

The customer-facing offers should use clear cultural language while the legal records retain exact internal terminology:

| Customer language | Internal/legal record | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Limited Release License — Edition 2 of 5 | Numbered Foundation License | One named release consumes one slot in the disclosed edition |
| Campaign Extension | Campaign License | Same edition slot, expanded merch/social/motion/team scope |
| Exclusive Music License | Music-Field Exclusive License | Signed exclusive instrument, possible only before any numbered license is sold |

Never create separate hidden edition pools for Campaign and Limited Release buyers. Both consume from the same master edition count.

Show three different promises separately:

- **Foundation scarcity:** only three or five music releases may license the underlying foundation.
- **Exact-export distinction:** the buyer's registered final export will not be intentionally reissued.
- **Legal exclusivity:** a separate signed exclusive license available only before any limited license sells.

Do not compress these into “100% unique.”

The license chooser asks about intended use rather than presenting a manipulative pricing ladder:

1. Is this for one single, EP, or album?
2. Will the image be used on merchandise or physical products?
3. Do you need motion, campaign formats, or label/team access?

Recommend an offer from those answers, allow override, never preselect the highest tier, and never add “best value” without a use-based explanation.

Create a real short reservation only when the visitor chooses **Continue to secure checkout**. A timer appears only while a database lock truly exists. If it expires, state that calmly.

### Cover Studio: an art director's desk

The Studio starts from the licensed artwork, not a blank prompt. It is neither Photoshop nor Midjourney.

Four customer modes:

1. **Direct** — describe the change, choose target, preserve elements, add role-labeled references, see exact cost, generate one or more separate takes.
2. **Type** — exact vector artist/title typography, crop, alignment, hierarchy, guides, safe areas, and advisory marks; zero credits.
3. **Preview** — streaming thumbnail, player, sleeve, poster, and social formats; motion later.
4. **Release** — preflight, final lock, license association, exports, certificate, and provenance.

The natural-language instruction is labeled **Director's Note**, not “Chat with AI.” Its interpretation is visible and editable through the controls below.

Default Direct controls:

- **Change** — the plain-language instruction;
- **Target** — region or semantic object;
- **Preserve** — protected subject, face, structure, palette, signature texture;
- **Reference** — optional image with an explicit role;
- **Scope** — `Refine`, `Reinterpret`, or `Depart` when edit permissions allow;
- **Takes** — number of separate output files;
- **Cost** — exact credits and post-run balance.

Advanced masks, reference strength, provider/model details, seed if any, hashes, and evaluation data live behind progressive disclosure.

Desktop anatomy:

- quiet top mode bar;
- collapsible left take rail;
- dominant centered canvas;
- contextual right inspector;
- minimal job/status strip;
- distraction-free mode that hides panels without hiding undo, selection, or save state.

The working canvas remains unchanged until a generated take passes validation. Each take creates a child version. Selecting a take never deletes its siblings. Editing a delivered version creates a new branch without invalidating the prior licensed export.

### Credits without casino behavior

- A Limited Release License should test including 12 Studio Credits—approximately four standard 3-credit masked edits.
- Typography, crop, layout, comparison, undo, QA, and standard export cost zero.
- State the cost before each run and the balance after it.
- Failed, blocked, malformed, canceled-before-provider-work, or mandatory-QA-failed jobs release the hold automatically.
- Purchased credits do not expire and do not auto-refill.
- Do not advertise giant arbitrary balances. Translate every balance into likely outcomes.
- Offer additional packs after the customer has experienced the included workflow, not as an aggressive checkout upsell.

The artwork license and credit wallet remain legally and technically separate.

### Motion grammar

Use motion as editing punctuation:

| Motion | Timing | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Focus/hover response | 80–140ms | Confirm target and state |
| Panel/drawer | 180–260ms | Reveal context without losing canvas |
| Shared Sleeve transition | 360–560ms | Preserve artwork continuity across routes |
| Take arrival | 240–360ms | Establish parent/child relationship |
| Verified registration | ≤900ms | Mark a real transactional consequence |

Rules:

- one atmospheric motion layer at most;
- no scroll hijacking, compulsory intro, or cinematic loader;
- pointer/wheel/touch input interrupts animation immediately;
- never delay price, rights, checkout, or editor controls for a reveal;
- no WebGL on every tile;
- use opacity/transform rather than expensive layout animation where possible;
- honor `prefers-reduced-motion` and supply an in-product motion toggle;
- the reduced-motion version retains all information and state changes.

### Sound grammar

Sound is material to a music product but must remain user-controlled.

- No autoplay music, background ambience, or surprise interface sounds.
- Phase 1 uses no audio ingestion.
- Phase 2 may accept a private excerpt only under a commercially cleared analyzer/provider and explicit retention controls.
- If the user explicitly enables interface sound later, limit the palette to four quiet physical cues: paper movement, selection, version lock, and edition stamp. Avoid vinyl crackle, cinematic whooshes, and sound on routine hover.
- If the user presses play, the active artwork may respond with restrained grain, light, or palette energy. Do not move layout, text, price, or controls to the beat.
- Stop/mute remains persistent and keyboard-accessible.
- The site remains complete in silence.

### Responsive composition

**Desktop**

- central artwork/canvas dominates;
- take rail and inspector can coexist;
- panels collapse for focus;
- precise mask and type controls available.

**Tablet**

- canvas remains dominant;
- versions and inspector become mutually exclusive drawers;
- compare becomes tap-and-hold or full-screen split;
- never squeeze three columns into narrow widths.

**Mobile**

- full-width/full-bleed square canvas;
- bottom modes: `Canvas`, `Direct`, `Takes`, `Release`;
- accessible bottom sheets for controls;
- swipe between separate takes, with visible previous/next alternatives;
- tap-and-hold before/after plus a labeled compare control;
- text changes, semantic targeting, licensing, checkout, review, and export remain complete;
- fine brush masking and pixel-level layout may be labeled **Desktop recommended** instead of being badly miniaturized;
- no hover-dependent information or gesture-only action.

### Copy and voice

Preferred verbs: `Explore`, `Audition`, `License`, `Secure`, `Direct`, `Use this take`, `Lock`, `Release`, `Download`.

Avoid: `Generate magic`, `Unleash creativity`, `Own forever`, `100% unique`, `AI-powered everything`, `Mint`, `Drop a prompt`, or technical provider names in customer-facing primary copy.

The experience can sound cultural and editorial in discovery, but rights, credits, payment, privacy, failure, and export language must become progressively plainer.

### Performance, accessibility, and experience budgets

- Public Archive routes are server-rendered/static where possible; do not ship editor or AI SDK code there.
- Use responsive AVIF/WebP previews; load protected originals only when authorized and necessary.
- Preserve every square's dimensions to prevent layout shift.
- Virtualize large archive walls and prefetch only the next likely work.
- Run canvas analysis off the main thread.
- Long jobs survive navigation and never hold the interface behind a full-screen spinner.
- Target p75 LCP ≤2.5s, INP ≤200ms, and CLS ≤0.1. [web.dev Web Vitals](https://web.dev/articles/vitals)
- Meet WCAG 2.2 AA. Provide visible focus, logical focus return, Escape-to-close, undo/redo, autosave recovery, practical 44px touch targets, and non-color state cues. [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)
- Every drag or gesture has a labeled alternative.
- Structured region/object selection provides an alternative to brush-only masking.
- Motion responds to `prefers-reduced-motion`; W3C notes that interaction-triggered motion can distract or cause nausea for some users. [W3C reduced-motion technique](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/css/C39)
- Artwork descriptions explain composition, mood, major subjects, and title space rather than repeating filenames.

### Non-negotiable anti-patterns

- generic card-grid homepage;
- standard SaaS hero followed by feature cards and pricing cards;
- purple/blue AI gradients, floating neon orbs, or glassmorphism;
- giant pill controls and emoji icons;
- template dashboard sidebar on public pages;
- chat-only editor;
- provider/model picker as the main experience;
- endless synthetic feed overwhelming Sergey's originals;
- generated title text treated as final typography;
- custom cursor that hides or harms the native pointer;
- permanent price/scarcity overlays on art;
- fake countdowns, watchers, demand, collectors, reviews, or sold counts;
- fake generation progress or payment success;
- forced subscription during artwork checkout;
- hidden credits, auto-refill, or expiring purchased credits;
- source art or fake portfolio work substituted when Sergey's files are missing;
- a mobile editor that is merely the desktop layout reduced until it becomes unusable.

### Design concept gate before implementation

Produce one coherent direction, not several style options. Render fresh standalone concept screens—one prompt, one screen, one file—for the desktop Threshold, Archive Focus, Sleeve Room, Rights Receipt, Studio, Vault, and one mobile composition. Use Sergey's real artwork once provided. Do not use one giant compressed board, collage, contact sheet, or mood board as the implementation reference.

Before coding, lock an implementation inventory: exact visible copy; approved artwork/media for every state; first-viewport composition; color tokens; font licenses/roles; grid/spacing; container model; icon inventory; component variants; hover/focus/selected/disabled states; motion timings; responsive recomposition; and the allowed above-the-fold copy list. After approval, browser screenshots must be compared directly with the concepts at desktop and mobile sizes, and every mismatch must be fixed or explicitly justified before sign-off.

## 10. Rights-aware artwork ingestion

Before an artwork can be published, record:

- stable asset ID and checksum;
- original/RAW/scan/project file;
- creator, date, medium, and publication history;
- copyright ownership—not merely file possession;
- contributor agreements or assignments;
- stock photo, texture, font, brush, and 3D-asset licenses;
- model/property releases;
- trademarks, logos, tattoos, packaging, and nested artwork review;
- AI involvement, provider/model/date, input rights, and human decisions;
- previous licenses, sales, clients, and restrictions;
- territory or usage limits.

Classify:

- **Green / exclusive eligible** — wholly owned, materially human-authored, no conflict.
- **Amber / HOLD** — not publishable until every component's commercial derivative-use and sublicensing rights are documented. After review, publish only as “artist-led, rights-reviewed,” with component-specific disclosures. Reserve the “verified human-authored” badge and exclusive eligibility for Green works. Block any stock/nonexclusive term that prohibits resale, sublicensing, or exclusivity.
- **Red / blocked** — uncertain ownership, missing release, restricted stock/font, or prior exclusive grant.

The public preview must be watermarked or resolution-limited. Never expose an unlicensed source master. Default delivery is flattened. If a layered source is included, remove or outline nontransferable fonts and assets and attach a component-license manifest.

Rights state machine:

`DRAFT → RIGHTS_REVIEW → AVAILABLE_LIMITED or AVAILABLE_1OF1 → RESERVED → LICENSED_SLOT or SOLD_EXCLUSIVE → RETIRED`

The exclusive order flow requires an atomic reservation with a visible short timer. Payment alone creates a pending order; activate the exclusive license only after a verified payment event and Sergey's signed counsel-approved instrument. Preserve the exact accepted license version forever.

## 11. Release delivery standard

Static package:

- highest-supported archival master, ideally 6000×6000 when source quality permits;
- 3000×3000 sRGB PNG;
- 3000×3000 high-quality sRGB JPG;
- text and textless versions;
- thumbnail QA sheet as a separate review document, not an artwork collage;
- license PDF;
- machine-readable provenance/export manifest;
- font/third-party component manifest when relevant.

Apple recommends square RGB JPG/PNG cover art around 3000×3000 and rejects promotional material and CMYK. [Apple asset guide](https://help.apple.com/itc/videoaudioassetguide/en.lproj/static.html)

Spotify currently requires square TIFF/PNG/JPG using lossless encoding, 640–10,000 pixels, and sRGB, and advises against upscaling. [Spotify cover requirements](https://support.spotify.com/us/artists/article/cover-art-requirements/)

Use **“exported to current published specifications,”** not “guaranteed approved.”

Motion add-ons:

- Spotify Canvas: 3–8 seconds, 9:16, 720–1080 px tall, MP4/JPG. [Spotify Canvas guidelines](https://support.spotify.com/us/artists/article/canvas-guidelines/)
- Apple Music motion: continuity with the static cover, start frame matching the cover, no unrelated cuts, and platform-specific 1:1/3:4 delivery. [Apple Music Album Motion Guidelines](https://help.apple.com/itc/albummotionguide/en.lproj/static.html)

Prefer deterministic motion—parallax, masked texture, light, type, looped camera, particles—before expensive generative video. It preserves the visual family and makes revision predictable.

## 12. ChatGPT Sites architecture

Use the Sites capability path because this is a multi-route application with durable data, uploads, identity, and external services.

### Platform shape

- Vinext/React application hosted by Sites.
- D1 for structured product, rights, project, job, order, license, and credit state.
- R2 for masters, previews, uploads, masks, generated versions, motion files, and exports.
- Public catalog routes remain anonymous.
- Studio and customer records require authenticated, server-verified identity.

Sites can use Sign in with ChatGPT for protected routes. If the product must support normal public email/Google sign-in for customers without ChatGPT, confirm the current supported public-auth path before implementing it; do not quietly invent an unsafe custom auth stack.

### D1 tables

`users`, `artworks`, `artwork_assets`, `rights_records`, `visual_dna`, `edit_permissions`, `editions`, `projects`, `project_members`, `project_versions`, `generation_jobs`, `generation_references`, `typography_layers`, `credit_accounts`, `credit_ledger`, `orders`, `licenses`, `license_acceptances`, `exports`, `provenance_events`, `audit_events`.

### R2 layout

```text
masters/{artwork_id}/source/*
previews/{artwork_id}/*
projects/{project_id}/inputs/*
projects/{project_id}/versions/{version_id}/*
projects/{project_id}/masks/*
exports/{license_id}/*
rights/{artwork_id}/*
```

Use short-lived signed access for private assets. API keys, Stripe secrets, and provider credentials stay server-side.

### Generation lifecycle

`QUOTED → CREDIT_HELD → POLICY_APPROVED → SUBMITTED → PROCESSING → INGESTED → QA → DELIVERED → CREDIT_SETTLED`

Failure routes end in `BLOCKED`, `PROVIDER_FAILED`, `QA_FAILED`, or `CANCELED`, with the hold released exactly once.

Long premium generations should be asynchronous. Sites can be the storefront and control plane; if the platform's current runtime cannot guarantee durable background execution/queues for provider jobs, connect a small external job runner rather than pretending a long browser request is reliable.

### Payments

- Stripe Checkout Sessions for one-time licenses and packs.
- Stripe Billing + Checkout for optional subscriptions.
- Stripe Customer Portal for plan, cancellation, and payment-method management.
- Signed, idempotent webhook processing.
- Issue ordinary nonexclusive licenses after payment confirmation, not after a client-side success redirect; activate a Music-Field Exclusive License only after both payment and Sergey's signed counsel-approved instrument.
- Bake mandatory fees into the displayed price.
- If outside designers are added later, use Stripe Connect Accounts v2 and explicitly choose responsibility, dashboard, requirements, and charge type. Do not add marketplace payouts in phase one.

Private Sites checkpoints are for owner review only and may block anonymous visitors and third-party callbacks. During private review, use Stripe test fixtures or polling and never claim webhook verification. Enable live checkout only after an explicitly approved public/shared deployment and confirmation that `/api/stripe/webhook` is publicly reachable without ChatGPT sign-in while still enforcing Stripe signature verification. If route-level public access is unavailable, host webhook and job callbacks in an external public worker.

## 13. Build phases

### Phase 0 — source and rights preparation

- Select the strongest 40–75 local works.
- Digitize/normalize masters without upscaling weak sources.
- Complete the rights matrix.
- Define edit permissions and limited-edition counts.
- Create visual DNA and watermarked preview assets.
- Test the offer and language with 8–12 independent artists/managers.

### Phase 1 — single-vendor commercial MVP

- One approved visual concept system covering Threshold, Archive, Sleeve Room, Rights Receipt, Studio, Vault, and mobile before frontend implementation.
- START WITH ART threshold plus finite Archive Wall/Focus discovery and persistent Curator Sentence.
- Shared Sleeve route continuity, Sleeve Room, Name-on-Art, Proofing Rail/Truth Thumbnail, and Artist Boundary.
- Limited Release License checkout in Stripe test mode; keep the Exclusive Music License as a signed, manually reviewed pending-order flow.
- Rights Receipt, genuine reservation state, 12 included credits, and verified Edition Seal behavior.
- Customer project/license/download Vault.
- One production-quality rights-aware masked-edit Take workflow through one provider adapter; add one tested fallback or human recovery path before public paid launch.
- Source Master/Take/Final Cut history, separate exact typography, credit ledger, QA, and standard export pack.
- 40–75 verified works; no third-party seller marketplace.

### Phase 2 — intelligent discovery and team workflow

- Commercially licensed, privacy-first song analysis and Visual DNA matching.
- Image-reference search.
- Approval rooms, comments, blind A/B voting.
- Campaign license, social kit, Canvas/motion exports.
- Provider routing benchmarks and margin optimization.

### Phase 3 — invitation-only marketplace

- Vetted external creators.
- Seller provenance review and contracts.
- Stripe Connect v2 onboarding/payouts.
- DMCA workflow, seller reserves, dispute handling.
- Contributor credit and revenue splits.

Do not begin with phase three. Single-vendor phase one avoids seller KYC, payout/tax operations, marketplace infringement, and quality inconsistency while the licensing engine is being proven.
